Bài đọc thực chiến 1 — Passage 1: E-Sports Teams in High Schools

Áp dụng Bản đồ Skim 60 giây để xác định hệ văn bản, sau đó Scan đúng vùng điểm nóng để trả lời 3 câu hỏi.

Paragraph 1: Competitive video gaming, widely known as e-sports, has rapidly entered high school campuses, sparking lively debate among educators, parents, and health professionals. While varsity e-sports leagues now operate in hundreds of schools worldwide, opinions remain sharply divided over whether competitive gaming belongs in an academic environment.

Paragraph 2: Supporters argue that organized e-sports offer benefits similar to traditional sports. Coach David Miller states, "Students learn teamwork, strategic communication, and emotional control under pressure." Furthermore, school counselors observe that gaming clubs often engage introverted teenagers who previously felt disconnected from school life, even opening pathways to university scholarships.

Paragraph 3: Conversely, critics contend that school-sponsored gaming sends the wrong message. Pediatrician Dr. Sarah Lin warns that excessive screen exposure contributes to sleep deprivation, eye strain, and sedentary habits. Additionally, some parents worry that competitive gaming may distract students from homework and blur the boundary between healthy recreation and gaming addiction.

Bài đọc thực chiến 2 — Passage 2: Teen Sleep & Nighttime Screen Use

Áp dụng kỹ thuật chia vùng nguồn khảo sát và đối chiếu con số – đối tượng – từ giới hạn để trả lời các câu hỏi.

Paragraph 1: Recent international surveys reveal a strong link between late-night digital device use and declining sleep duration among adolescents. A 2025 report by the National Sleep Foundation in the United States found that 72% of high school students keep smartphones inside their bedrooms at night, and nearly half check notifications after midnight on school nights.

Paragraph 2: The US data also highlights a sharp contrast between heavy and light screen users. Teenagers who spent more than three hours on screens after 8:00 PM averaged just 5.8 hours of sleep on weekdays, compared to 7.9 hours among peers who turned off devices an hour before bed. On weekends, however, both groups slept over nine hours to recover from weekday exhaustion.

Paragraph 3: Similar patterns emerged in a separate study by the Adolescent Health Institute in South Korea. Among 4,500 surveyed students aged 15 to 17, online gaming and short-video apps accounted for 68% of late-night screen time, whereas educational reading represented under 9%. Researchers concluded that nighttime entertainment, rather than homework, is the primary driver of teen sleep loss.
